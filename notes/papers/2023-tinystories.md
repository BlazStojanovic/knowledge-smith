# TinyStories: How Small Can Language Models Be and Still Speak Coherent English?

## Citation

- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.07759
- Authors: Ronen Eldan, Yuanzhi Li (Microsoft Research)
- Year / venue: arXiv preprint, May 2023
- Raw PDF: [[raw/papers/pdf/2023-tinystories]]

- Grounding axis: [[maps/grounding/ungrounded-seed]] — vocabulary- and feature-conditioned seed prompts (Axis 1)

## Core Claim

A purely synthetic dataset of short stories using only the vocabulary a 3–4-year-old would understand, generated by GPT-3.5/GPT-4, lets sub-10M-parameter models — and even single-transformer-block models — produce fluent multi-paragraph stories with grammar, factual coherence, and basic reasoning. Existing 125M-parameter pre-trained baselines (GPT-Neo small, GPT-2 small) trained on web-scale corpora cannot match this on the same task.

## Key Paper Ideas

- **Refined corpus, not refined model.** The hypothesis is that SLMs fail on web-scale corpora because of the corpora's breadth/diversity, not because of architectural limits. Restricting *content domain* (3-4yr-old stories) and *lexical domain* (~1500 basic words) preserves grammar+reasoning while removing the world-knowledge tax.
- **Vocabulary-triple seeding for diversity.** Each generation samples one verb + one noun + one adjective from ~1500-word lists; the model is told to weave them in. This combinatorial seed forces wide coverage and prevents the "high-temperature still collapses to a few stories" failure mode of naive bulk prompting (paper §2).
- **Feature-conditioning for variety.** A second random subset is drawn from {dialogue, plot twist, bad ending, moral value, foreshadowing, conflict} and injected into the prompt. Stylistic variety as an explicit axis of the seed.
- **TinyStories-Instruct variant.** Each story prepended with a random subset of: word list, required sentence, feature list, 1–2-line summary. Provides a held-out instruction-following test bed; an OOD split disallows summary+words combinations to test compositional generalisation.
- **GPT-Eval rubric.** ~50 manually-prepared story beginnings, ask the trained SLM to complete; ask GPT-4 to grade Grammar / Creativity / Consistency (and Plot / Instruct-fidelity for the Instruct variant) and *guess the student's age group*. Multidimensional, free-form, harder to game than match-exact benchmarks.
- **Width vs depth findings.** Hidden dimension matters more for factual knowledge; layer count matters more for context tracking. Grammar saturates earliest; consistency emerges next; creativity continues improving with scale and continues to lag GPT-4.
- **Polynomial scaling law at small scale.** For a fixed FLOP budget, optimal `(layers, hidden_dim)` choices fall on a power law — same shape as Hoffmann et al / Kaplan et al at 100M–10B scale, suggesting universality of the law rather than a corpus-specific accident.
- **Interpretability gain from smallness.** With one transformer block + 16 heads, distinct heads cleanly separate into distance-based (positional) and semantic (entity-tracking) families; MLP neurons in small models pick up human-readable roles (pronouns-as-subject, adjectives, "introduce-the-protagonist"). GPT2-XL neurons under the same probe look uninterpretable.

## Methodology

- **Generator stack**: GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 via prompt only. No fine-tuning of the generator.
- **Seed assembly**: random `(verb, noun, adjective)` triple from ~1500-word lists + random subset of feature tags + instruction "use very simple words a 3-year-old would understand". TinyStories-Instruct adds a back-derived summary + extracted sentence.
- **Trained models**: GPT-Neo architecture, window 256, context 512, GPT-Neo tokenizer truncated to top-10K tokens. Hidden dims ∈ {64, 128, 256, 512, 768, 1024}, layers ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 12}. Smallest models ~1M params, largest ~80M, all trainable in <30h on a single V100.
- **Evaluation**: GPT-Eval as above. For diversity / memorization, the four-headed Rouge-$k$ audit summarised in [[#Diversity / memorization audit (§4.4.1)]].
- **Memorization tiering**: Three classes — exact, simple template (entities swapped), complex template (abstract pattern reuse). Paper claims models avoid the first two by these metrics; flags complex template matching as unmeasurable.

### Diversity / memorization audit (§4.4.1)

The paper's diversity claim rests on a single primitive — Rouge-$k$ overlap (precision $R_{k,p}$ and fmeasure $R_k$, see [[metrics/rouge-overlap]]) — applied against four different reference sets. Reporting all four is the recipe; each answers a distinct question:

| Symbol | Reference set | Plot | Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $s_i := R_{2,p}(T_i, T'_i)$ | the original training-set continuation $T'_i$ for the same prompt | Fig 14 | "Is the generation a copy of the story it 'should have' produced?" |
| $r_i := \max_{j \ne i} R_2(T_i, T_j)$ | the other 99 model generations | Fig 15 | "Are the generations collapsing onto each other?" (mode-collapse) |
| $g_r$ = corpus frequency of generated $k$-gram $r$ | full training corpus, log-scale histogram | Fig 16 | "Are typical 4-/5-grams in the output rare or unseen in training?" |
| $h_i := \max_{j \in [m]} R_{2,p}(T_i, S_j)$ | every story $S_j$ in the entire training set | Fig 17 | "What is the closest training point to this generation?" |

Protocol: 100 training stories, each cut at ~40% of their length and used as the prompt; generate one completion per prompt; compute the four quantities above for each model size.

The four together rule out exact + simple-template memorization; the paper acknowledges they cannot rule out *complex template matching* (paraphrase-level reuse with no shared $k$-grams).

This Rouge-overlap-against-multiple-references pattern is reusable beyond TinyStories — see [[metrics/rouge-overlap]] §Aggregation patterns.

## Experiments

- Sweep over hidden ∈ {64,128,256,512,768,1024} × layers ∈ {1,2,4,8,12} on TinyStories and TinyStories-Instruct; comparison baselines GPT-Neo 125M, GPT-2 small/medium/large (125M–774M), GPT-2 XL (1.5B), and GPT-4 as upper-bound.
- Eval suite: 50-prompt completion eval (Grammar/Creativity/Consistency), Instruct eval (50 instruction-sets), Plot-coherence eval, factual / reasoning / context-tracking targeted prompt sets (Figs 9–11).
- Diversity / memorization audit: see [[#Diversity / memorization audit (§4.4.1)]] above for the four Rouge-$k$ configurations.
- Architecture-search experiments: hyperparameter sweep over heads ∈ {2,4,8} at fixed `(hidden=768, layers∈{1,2})`. Scaling-law experiment: lowest-eval-loss point per FLOP budget across the full hidden×layers grid.

## Key Results

- **Coherence emerges below 10M params.** A 28M-parameter, 8-layer model produces multi-paragraph stories that GPT-4 grades comparably to GPT-2 XL (1.5B); a 21M / 1-layer model still produces grammatical text and partial reasoning (Figs 1, 2, 6).
- **Grammar / Consistency saturate near GPT-4 (normalised score ≈ 0.93 / 0.90 at 12 layers, hidden 1024); Creativity plateaus around 0.77 — a persistent gap from GPT-4's 1.00.** Source: Fig 4 normalised scores.
- **Width vs depth.** Knowledge prompts (Fig 9) improve fastest with hidden dim; context-tracking prompts (Fig 11) improve fastest with depth. 1-layer models can answer some factual prompts but cannot maintain entity continuity.
- **Instruction-following needs ≥2 layers**; 1-layer models score noticeably worse on Instruct/Plot than on Grammar/Consistency at matched hidden.
- **Diversity holds up under all four Rouge configurations** (see [[#Diversity / memorization audit (§4.4.1)]]). $s_i$ (Rouge-2 precision vs original ending) is centred well below 0.1 for every model size — generations are not copies of the "intended" continuation. $r_i$ (max pairwise Rouge-2) shows the 100 generations are mutually distinct rather than collapsed to a few templates. $g_r$ in log-scale shows most 4-/5-grams in generations never appear in the training corpus (Fig 16). $h_i$ (max precision vs full corpus) — closest training point is still far from any generation.
- **Polynomial FLOP↔model-size scaling holds at this scale** (Fig 23), with "best-model size grows as power of FLOPs" qualitatively matching Kaplan/Chinchilla shape despite ~3 orders of magnitude smaller.
- **More heads helps when head count is small.** At hidden=768 / layers=2, eval loss 1.38 → 1.34 → 1.33 going 2 → 4 → 8 heads; same direction at layers=1 (1.58 → 1.56 → 1.54).

## Relevance To Poolside

*Our interpretation, explicitly labelled.*

- TinyStories is a clean controlled experiment for the "refined synthetic corpus → small model can learn the *form*" claim — a low-budget analogue of phi-1's textbooks claim, but in story-domain rather than code-domain. For Poolside it matters as a *methodology benchmark*: the vocabulary-triple seeding trick is a candidate diversity lever for our own data pipelines, and the GPT-Eval rubric is a candidate evaluation paradigm where existing match-exact benchmarks miss.
- We infer: the same vocabulary-conditioning idea is unlikely to transfer cleanly to code. Code's "vocabulary" is API surface + library imports + control structures; mimicking the "3yr-old word list" framing would require a graded API surface that doesn't obviously exist. But the *combinatorial seed* idea — sample a small structural triple (operation, data-shape, error-mode), force the generator to synthesise a snippet using all three — is directly transferable and worth comparing to existing rephrasing pipelines.
- The width-vs-depth finding is suggestive but at this scale should not be load-bearing for any training-recipe decision; treat as motivating, not as evidence.

## Key Follow-Ups / Jumping-Off Points

- Compare the vocabulary-triple seed with [[concepts/diversity|diversity-via-conditioning]] tactics in Persona-Hub, AgentInstruct, and Genetic-Instruct — is there a unified "seed-axis cardinality × axis-count" predictor of dataset coverage?
- The GPT-Eval rubric is the same family of design as Cosmopedia's "GPT-4 grade three axes" pattern. Worth a row in [[maps/evaluation/landscape]].
- Test whether the "2 layers is enough for instruction-following" finding survives under more complex instructions (multi-step, external-knowledge-bearing). The instruction set here is single-step constraint-following, which understates depth requirements.
- The memorization audit explicitly admits *complex template matching* is unmeasured. This is the same limitation that hits [[notes/papers/2023-textbooks-are-all-you-need|phi-1]] and motivated subsequent contamination work — consider whether the 50-prompt eval set could be paraphrase-leaking.
- Sister paper: Eldan & Li's later work on synthetic textbooks (phi-1.5 was concurrent at MSR; check whether the GPT-Eval framework reappears).

## Caveats

- **Eval is GPT-4-judged.** Self-evident generator/grader correlation risk: GPT-4 judges output of small models trained on GPT-3.5/GPT-4 stories. The grade absolutely measures alignment-with-GPT-4-style-stories, only indirectly "speaks coherent English". Authors acknowledge but do not control for it.
- **Eval set is 50 prompts.** Tiny. Variance per score is reported (e.g. 6.66/0.71 = score/normalised) but the absolute number of evaluation items is small enough that single-paragraph differences move the average.
- **Memorization audit is Rouge-based.** Complex template matching is admitted to be unmeasurable; the strong Rouge-low result rules out exact + near-exact copying only.
- **Domain restriction is severe.** "3-year-old vocabulary, story format" is a much narrower task than "language modelling". The paper conflates "small models can do *this* task" with "small models can speak coherent English" in places. Direct generalisation to code, math, or factual QA is unsupported.
- **Single-V100 / 30h limits compute-matched comparisons.** The GPT-Neo 125M baseline was *not* re-trained on TinyStories; its scores reflect its original web-corpus pretraining, which is the contrast being argued. A fairer ablation would also train GPT-Neo 125M on TinyStories.
- **Polynomial scaling-law claim is suggestive.** Authors note that the data is "a bit small for the data to be very conclusive" (their words, §6). Treat the scaling-law section as a positive replication signal, not a quantitative result.
